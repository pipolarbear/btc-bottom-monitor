How the scores work

The monitor's main question is: is BTC cheap? It also asks whether the fall has stopped, and where we are in the cycle. Each has its own score, because the answers often differ. This page explains the method behind each one, with the backtest results and how far to trust them.

You can check every figure yourself: Check our homework works the backtest out in your browser from the published data, with the monitor's own code.

Three questions

Value: is BTC cheap?16 signals against the readings seen at past cycle lows. The main score.
Bottom in? Has the fall stopped?The timing check. 6 signs that only show after a low. It comes late by design.
Cycle clock: where are we?Time and depth now, against the 2015, 2018 and 2022 lows.

Read them together. Value alone acts too early: in 2022 it first read Cheap in June at about $22,300, then price fell a further 31% to $15,479 in November. Bottom in? alone acts too late: it reached 50 two to three months after each past low, when price was already 27–90% above it (+90% in 2015, +27% in 2019, +37% in 2023). Value says the price is good. Bottom in? says whether the fall has stopped. Both are scores, not probabilities.

Why two scores? Cheap is not the same as the low. In the weekly backtest, when the value score was 60–74 (Cheap), price was within 30% of that cycle's final low in only about a third to a half of the weeks. But BTC was higher one year later in 100% of those weeks. The value score is good at "is this a good price for a long hold?". It is weak at "is this the exact low?". Bottom in? fills that gap, but it only turns up weeks after the low.

Value: the thesis in five points

  1. A cycle low forms when sellers run out. Coins move from weak hands to strong hands, mostly at a loss. So the score looks for three things: price below what holders paid (cost basis), people selling at a loss (capitulation), and enough time since the top.
  2. No single signal is reliable. Every signal has false alarms. A weighted blend of different kinds of evidence gives fewer false alarms than any one signal.
  3. Weights reward a good record. A signal gets more weight if it fired at past lows and rarely fired at other times.
  4. Signals that measure the same thing share weight. MVRV, long-term holder cost and balanced price all come from realized-cost data, so together they do not get triple weight.
  5. Each signal scores on a straight line. 0 is "no signal". 100 is the reading seen at past lows. Past 100 it stays at 100, so one extreme reading cannot push the score alone.

How each weight was set

Each signal went through a weekly backtest from mid-2013 to November 2025, with three tests and one judgment:

  1. Hit. Did the signal score 70 or more within 90 days of the 2015, 2018 and 2022 cycle lows?
  2. Precision. Of all the days it scored 70 or more, what share were within 30% of a two-year low? A day picked at random scores 6%, so 30% is five times better than chance.
  3. Overlap. Does it measure the same thing as a signal already in the score? If yes, the two share weight.
  4. Judgment. Where there is no data to test (leverage data starts in 2023), the weight comes from judgment and stays small.

Small-cycle levels

Each cycle low has been less deep than the one before. MVRV at the low went from 0.56 (2015) to 0.69 (2018) to 0.75 (2022). If the score only gives full marks at the old levels, it can read too low at a shallower low. So the monitor has two views, and you choose in the Value card:

  1. Classic. Fixed full-score levels: the readings that all three past lows reached.
  2. Small-cycle (the default). For each signal, we take its most extreme reading within 60 days of each of the three lows. If the reading got less extreme at each low, we fit a straight line through the three lows. The full-score level then moves by one step of that line (the change per cycle). The 2022-cycle levels are the classic levels. The move is never more than half the gap to the zero level. In the backtest, each past cycle uses its own step of the line, so the history stays fair.

A cheap window runs from the first close with Value at 60 or more to the last one. A gap below 60 of up to 12 weeks does not end it. The card shows the week of this window next to how long each past window lasted, and how much further BTC fell after the first close in the window. How the monitor shows its working

  1. How we got the score. The Value and Bottom in? cards show each signal's points, which add up to the score. Each row's ? button gives the reading now and, for Bottom in?, the price that would raise it.
  2. Since last week. The summary line compares now with a past close (7 days ago, last Thursday or 4 weeks ago): both scores, the price, and any cheap window or new low in between.
  3. Price cross-check. The page compares the Coin Metrics daily close with the Bybit daily close for the last five days. A gap above 2% shows in the data line, because one feed may be wrong.
  4. Your layout and colours. Arrange moves and resizes cards. Colours changes the score colours: seven pairings, each checked for red-green colour blindness, or your own. Copy setup link carries your layout, colours and settings to another device. All of it stays in your browser.

Live data: where each number comes from

Each time the monitor opens, it pulls the data from the sources below and works out every score in your browser. It does not wait for a daily file. Nine value signals use price directly: CVDD, MVRV, long-term holder cost, balanced price, short-term holder cost, the 200-week and 200-day averages, Puell and reserve risk. Their lines move slowly, so the page divides each line into the live price every 15 seconds. The history chart and heat map use the close of each UTC day, worked out the same way.

How far to trust the backtest

  1. Three bottoms, not hundreds of tests. The Bottom in? backtest covers the 2015, 2018 and 2022 bear markets. Its band figures count weekly readings, and neighbouring weeks are nearly the same reading. "93% of 30 weeks" rests on three bear markets, so the monitor also says what happened in each one.
  2. Out-of-sample test. The small-cycle levels are fitted on all three lows. Hiding one low at a time and fitting the line on the other two changes little: the score at the 2015 low goes from 62 to 64, and the 2018 and 2022 results do not change. The classic levels and the weights were set with all three lows in view, which no test here can undo. Details in Check our homework.
  3. Scores, not probabilities. A Bottom in? reading of 70 does not mean a 70% chance that the low is in. Both scores are weighted checklists, set against past cycles.
  4. The live score is the tested model. Money flows are shown but not scored, because they have no data before 2024.
  5. Price checks give false starts on their own. The monitor also shows three plain price checks under Bottom in?: depth, a confirmed higher low, and trend (daily close above the 30-day average, 30-day above the 60-day). They are not scored. They show what has improved and the price that would undo it. Bitstamp daily data:

Higher low: after the cycle low, a bounce of at least 10%, a pullback of at least 5% that stays above the low, then a daily close above the bounce high. A new low resets it. This is why the monitor leads with Value: price checks say what price is doing, Value says whether the price is good. Both are needed.

Limits

  1. There are only three full cycle lows to test on (2015, 2018, 2022). Small samples can mislead.
  2. The small-cycle levels come from a line through three points. The deep-value check was set after this cycle's false start. Both are fitted to the same history they are tested on.
  3. The leverage signal has no backtest. Its weight is a judgment. Money flows cannot be backtested, so they are shown but not scored.
  4. ETFs and company treasuries now hold a large share of coins. Holder behaviour may change, so the deep on-chain readings of past lows may not come back.
  5. Data providers can revise past values. LookIntoBitcoin lines arrive one to two days late, and its charts do not all update at the same time. Between updates the page divides the last line into the current price.
  6. The CBBI on the monitor is a separate index by Colin Talks Crypto. It is a cross-check and is not part of these scores.
  7. Information only. Not financial advice. Past lows do not promise future lows.
